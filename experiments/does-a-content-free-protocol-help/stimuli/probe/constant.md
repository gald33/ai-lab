# probe/constant — for the constancy probe, not frozen

*The base instructions already state the fact: "Your capacities and tastes are
the same in every episode of this round, and so is everyone else's." This block
adds no new fact. It draws the implication — that what you learn about another
trader keeps its value — and says what to do with it. That is a domain
instruction, and a run using it may not be cited as evidence about
deliberation protocols.*

## What you learn keeps its value

Nothing about who is good at what changes between episodes. The trader who
could make salt cheaply in episode 1 can still make it cheaply in episode 9,
wants the same goods, and is the same trader you dealt with before. Only the
stocks reset.

So the first episodes are worth spending differently from the last. Early on
you know nothing about anyone, and finding out who makes what cheaply — by
trading with them, by watching what they produce, by asking — is worth more
than the trade itself. Later, that knowledge is the thing you have that a
stranger would not: you know who to go to for iron and what they will want back.

Two consequences worth holding on to:

- **A trade that went well will go well again.** If an exchange left you better
  off, the same exchange with the same trader is available every remaining
  episode, and it costs nothing to repeat.
- **A trade that went badly was not bad luck.** The rates you were offered
  reflect what things cost that trader to make, and those costs do not move. If
  a rate was poor once, it will be poor again unless you ask for a different one.

By the last episodes you should be repeating what works and no longer paying to
discover what you already know.
