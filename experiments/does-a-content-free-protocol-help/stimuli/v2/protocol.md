## A shared way of talking

The participants here all have the same communication tools, but no shared
habits for using them. The following is a convention several groups have found
useful. It is a suggestion, not a rule. Nothing checks whether you follow it,
and you may depart from it whenever you judge that better.

1. **Choose the channel by who needs to see it.** Publish where everyone can
   see it when broad visibility helps. Send directly when one particular
   participant's response is what matters.
2. **Make the addressee obvious.** Whoever reads a message should be able to
   tell immediately whether it is meant for them, for a specific other, or for
   everyone.
3. **Attach a reply to what it replies to.** Quote, name, or otherwise point at
   the message you are answering, rather than starting a fresh disconnected
   one. A conversation nobody can follow backwards stops being a conversation.
4. **Say what kind of thing you are saying**, when it is not obvious — whether
   you are asking, answering, clarifying, or summarising. A question that reads
   as a statement usually gets no reply.
5. **Leave room for a reply.** When a message needs an answer from someone,
   give them the chance to give one before moving the discussion on. Repeating
   or advancing before anyone can respond tends to fragment a thread rather
   than speed it up.
6. **Do not wait for everyone.** Only the participants a matter actually
   concerns need to weigh in. Waiting for silent parties who are not involved
   wastes time and gains nothing.
7. **Summarise when a thread gets hard to follow.** If a discussion has
   accumulated more than a few turns, restate where it currently stands — what
   is settled, what is open, who is waiting on whom.
8. **Close things that are finished.** When a conversation has done its job,
   say so and stop. Continuing past that point crowds out the threads that are
   still live.

None of this tells you what to say. It is only about how to say it so that a
group of participants who cannot see each other can still hold a conversation.
