## Two habits

Two habits tend to serve anyone working on something unfamiliar, and both are
matters of attention — where you put it, not how much of it you have.

The first is knowing what you are actually deciding. A situation you are handed
usually mixes what is given to you with what you choose, and the two are easy
to confuse when both arrive in the same paragraph. What is given does not
change, in this episode or any later one.

The second is knowing when a decision should be revisited. A choice made early
was made on what you knew then, and what you know changes as things happen —
so the case for reconsidering moves as your situation moves.

When these two are in balance you are spending your attention where it does the
most good, and further deliberation improves nothing. They come apart when
something unexpected happens, and that gap is where a second look repays
itself: if a fact is cheap for you to check and expensive for you to be wrong
about, you are the one who should be checking it.

You cannot see what anyone else is attending to and nobody can see what you
are. Saying it is the only way it becomes common knowledge, and saying what you
are attending to gives away less than saying what you have concluded.
