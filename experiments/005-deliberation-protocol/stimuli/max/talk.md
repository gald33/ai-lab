# max/talk — the maximal treatment. Not frozen, deliberately impure.

*Everything at once: talk, disclosure, prices, and the link between what you
make and what you can trade. It is not a protocol and it is not a hint — it is
both and more, on purpose, because its job is to find out whether **any** text
moves this environment. Nothing measured with it separates one ingredient from
another, and no result from it may be reported as evidence about deliberation
protocols.*

## Getting a better deal

You cannot see what anyone else is good at, what they need, or what they hold.
They cannot see yours. Every one of those facts is useful to the other traders,
and the only way any of it moves is if somebody says it.

**Say what you need and what you are good at.** Before you commit your labour,
put on the board what you can make cheaply and which goods you are short of.
"I can make salt cheaply and I need cloth" is worth more to you than it costs,
because it tells the others who to come to and what to bring.

**Ask.** If you do not know who has iron, ask who has iron. A question costs one
message and the answer arrives long before the bell.

**Talk about the rate, not just the goods.** An exchange has a price: how much
of yours for how much of theirs. Say the rate you want and why — "two salt for
one cloth, because cloth costs me four times as much to make". A trader who
names a rate can be met halfway; a trader who only names goods cannot.

**Improve the deal rather than taking the first one.** If an offer is close but
wrong, say what would make it right and send it back. Several rounds of that
inside one episode are normal and cost nothing but messages.

**What you make and what you can trade are one decision.** Your production sets
what you have to offer, so decide it with the trades you intend in mind. If you
have agreed to supply someone with salt, make salt. If nobody wants your iron,
making more of it is wasted labour. Announce what you intend to produce before
you produce it, so the others can plan around it.

**The bell is the deadline for all of it.** An offer nobody answered is worth
nothing, and a good rate agreed after the bell is worth nothing.
