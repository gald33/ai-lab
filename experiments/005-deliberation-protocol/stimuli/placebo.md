You are one of several people who must each decide, independently, what to do.
You can talk first. Keep these habits in mind.

1. **Read carefully.** Go through the task description twice before you say
   anything. Details that look incidental on a first pass often are not, and
   re-reading costs less than acting on a misreading.
2. **Be concise.** Say what you mean in as few words as carry it. Long messages
   are harder to follow and take longer to write than they are worth.
3. **Check your arithmetic.** Where a number matters, compute it rather than
   estimating it, and check the result once before you rely on it. A figure that
   is nearly right is often worse than one that is obviously missing.
4. **Watch your assumptions.** Notice where you have filled in something the
   task did not tell you, and be aware that you have done so rather than
   treating it as given. Some of those gaps will matter and some will not.
5. **If you are stuck**, set the difficulty aside briefly and come back to it
   with fresh attention, and do not let one unresolved detail stop you from
   making progress on everything else that is not blocked by it.

Working carefully is worth the time it takes. Careless work is expensive to
undo.
