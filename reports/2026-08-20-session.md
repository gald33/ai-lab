## The findings, in the order they matter

### 1. Most of 002's ruin was the accounting, not the convention

Arm C: 14/24 stock-ruined → **1/24** permanently ruined under per-period
consumption. Arm D: 18/24 → **0/24**. Intervals do not overlap.

The islands 002 reported as ruined were not hopeless economies — they were
economies where one bad first commitment was fatal, and they are worth ~0.75
(above the exchange ceiling of 0.521) the moment commitment stops being
permanent.

**But the arm ordering survives**, so 002's ladder was measuring conventions
after all. Those two together are more useful than either: the ladder measured
conventions, the ruin measured the world.

→ [004 report](2026-08-20-004-stock-and-flow.md)

### 2. No ordinary promotion rule can promote a better protocol

When a candidate's payoff scales with how many others use it, `greedy`, `nmin`
and `interval` promote **zero times in 120 replications each**, and score
identically to a control that declines to promote by construction. Under a
coordination payoff, automated promotion and no promotion are the same policy.

The single exception is the rule that never runs a split population: UCB sends a
whole step to one candidate, so every trial runs at full adoption. The design
predicted a population-wide switch would be *necessary*; the run produced one
unprompted, as the only thing that works — under a harness where switching is
free, which is exactly what a real migration violates.

→ [003 report](2026-08-20-003-promotion-rules.md)

### 3. A convention's error kills islands without denting efficiency

Across 48 islands per cell and the entire δ sweep — 121 surviving islands —
survivor efficiency has median 0.972 and range 0.741–1.000, while survival goes
23/48 → 0/48. The whole effect of content error is on *whether an island
survives*, and it is binary.

This invalidated the tier's own headline readout: δ\* has to be defined on
survival, not efficiency, or it would have been estimated from a line that does
not move.

Sharpening a price is far worse than flattening it, and the gap is at errors too
small to notice: at a matched realised error of 0.019, flattening leaves 19/48
alive and sharpening 7/48.

→ [002 Tier 3 report](2026-08-20-002-tier3-calibration.md)

### 4. What this says about the lab's standing question

"Conventions are critical, especially to newborn agents" now has a shape it did
not have this morning:

- The convention's value is in **the price it reaches inside an episode**, not
  in anything carried across episodes. Starving within-period discovery takes
  arm C's permanent ruin from 1/24 to 12/24; nothing measurable carries between
  periods. For a newborn agent, what matters is how fast the convention
  converges in the episode it is actually in.
- **Protocols and strategies need different promotion machinery.** Strategies
  can be competed per-candidate; protocols cannot be competed at all without a
  population-wide switch, because a protocol under trial is not shared and
  therefore underperforms *because* it is being trialled.
- The distribution axis — the CS − CP gap, the actual claim about sharedness —
  **is not measurable by scripted agents at any δ**. It is irreducibly a
  model-tier question. The paid tier is not a more realistic version of the free
  one; it is the only place that axis exists.

## Three results that were wrong before they were right

Recorded because the reviewing question is not "are these findings right" but
"is this process one whose findings can be trusted", and the honest answer
depends on these.

### The pool that was never sampled

003's first run produced a complete set of confident, entirely false tables.
Largest-remainder allocation with a fixed tie-break gave every challenger an
identical remainder, so the same low indices took the spare invocations on every
step and **the rest of the pool was never tried at all** — any rule, any mode,
whole stream. The tell was the best candidate's traffic share reading exactly
`0.0`.

The first fix — rotate by one position per step — was also wrong, leaving a
240/240/160/160 split. The working fix rotates within the contested tie group.
Two gates pin it, including one asserting the unrotated version still starves.

### The channel with nothing left to carry

004's convergence measurement read as "agents do not learn from a shared price".
Before reporting it, the learning channel was inspected directly: it reaches the
true equilibrium **inside period 0**, every agent agreeing to machine precision.
Nothing was left for a later period to learn. Convergence was not absent, it was
unmeasurable — a finding about the measurement before it is a finding about the
island.

### The island picked by eye

Having built a knob to make convergence measurable, the starved regime produced
a pooled downward drift and one island descending 3, 4, 2, 0, 1, 0 — exactly the
wanted result. Paired per island it evaporates (p = 0.45 to 0.83). The drift was
correlated agent-periods; the clean island was one of twenty-four, chosen after
the fact.

**A fourth, found while writing these reports:** `close_period` counted offers
still open at the bell as *rejections*, inflating a flow run's rejection count
relative to a stock run's by construction. No reported finding used the metric.
Fixed, separated, gated.

## Where a reviewer should push hardest

Ranked across all three experiments. Each experiment report has its own list.

1. **004's homogeneity argument** (`Σα = 1` ⇒ `T` periods ↔ `T ×` frontier). If
   this is wrong, flow and stock are not on one axis and finding 1 collapses.
2. **003's `allocate` tie-group rotation.** This function silently decided the
   first run's entire result. It is now gated, but by gates the same author
   wrote.
3. **The 002 benchmark correction.** A published number was changed.
4. **003's free switching assumption.** The bandit result — the only positive
   result in that experiment — rests on a zero-cost population-wide switch.
5. **004's `close_period` ordering**, on which the "invariant is as strong under
   flow" claim rests.
6. **Whether survival is the right primary readout** in 002's Tier 3, or whether
   an all-island-defined welfare measure would carry more than a binary.
7. **The cross-experiment import** in 004 — first shared dependency in a repo
   that avoids frameworks on purpose.
